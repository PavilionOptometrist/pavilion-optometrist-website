Refractive Conditions

See how small focusing changes can make a big difference to how you see.

Presbyopia

Age-related loss of near focus, typically starting around age 40. The eye's lens gradually loses flexibility.

Presbyopia is an age-related condition in which the eye gradually loses its ability to focus on nearby objects. It typically begins around the age of 40 and results from the natural aging process affecting the lens inside the eye.

✅ Treatment: Reading glasses, bifocals, progressive lenses, or multifocal contact lenses.

Astigmatism

An irregular curvature of the cornea or lens causing blurred or distorted vision at all distances.

Astigmatism is a common refractive error caused by an irregularly shaped cornea or lens. This prevents light from focusing properly on the retina, resulting in blurred or distorted vision at all distances.

✅ Treatment: Prescription glasses, toric contact lenses, or refractive surgery.

Myopia

Myopia (short-sightedness) occurs when distant objects focus short inside the eye, resulting in blurred distance vision.

Myopia (Short-sightedness) is a refractive condition where light focuses in front of the retina, making distant objects appear blurry while near objects remain clear.

✅ Treatment: Prescription glasses, contact lenses, or orthokeratology.

Hyperopia

Hyperopia (far-sightedness) occurs when the eye's focal strength is less than normal, making close-up vision difficult.

Hyperopia (Far-sightedness) is a refractive condition where light focuses behind the retina, making near objects appear blurry while distant objects may remain clear.

✅ Treatment: Prescription glasses, contact lenses, or refractive surgery.

Emmetropia

Emmetropia describes an eye that is perfectly in focus — no refractive error and no optical intervention is required.

Emmetropia is the perfect refractive state of the eye. Light rays entering the eye focus precisely on the retina, providing clear vision at all distances without the need for corrective lenses.

✅ Regular checkups help maintain this healthy state.

Eye Conditions & Diseases

Understanding common eye conditions and their treatments.

Mature Cataract

A dense clouding of the lens inside the eye. After removing the cataract, the best corrected vision is...

A mature cataract is a dense clouding of the lens inside the eye that significantly affects vision. The lens becomes opaque and hard, making it difficult to see clearly.

✅ Treatment: Surgical removal of the cataract and replacement with an artificial lens (IOL).

Early Cataract

An early cataract is a slight clouding of the lens inside the eye. A haziness in vision may result during low light or at night...

An early cataract is a slight clouding of the lens that may cause glare, halos, or haziness in low light conditions. Regular monitoring is recommended.

✅ Treatment: Updated prescription glasses, anti-glare coatings, or surgical removal if progression occurs.

Keratoconus

This is a slow progressive thinning of the cornea (front of the eye). Thinning causes visual distortions which make clear vision...

Keratoconus is a progressive thinning of the cornea that causes it to bulge into a cone-like shape, leading to distorted vision.

✅ Treatment: Specialty contact lenses (RGP, scleral), corneal cross-linking, or corneal transplant in severe cases.

Dry Eye

This very common condition is characterized by dry, scratchy and burning eyes as a result of systemic conditions...

Dry eye syndrome occurs when the eyes do not produce enough tears or produce poor-quality tears. Symptoms include burning, stinging, and a gritty sensation.

✅ Treatment: Artificial tears, prescription eye drops, lifestyle changes, or punctal plugs.

Glaucoma - Open Angle

Open angle glaucoma results from the optic nerve's inability to tolerate the pressure within the eye. Unchecked this...

Open angle glaucoma is the most common form of glaucoma, where the optic nerve is damaged by pressure buildup in the eye.

✅ Treatment: Prescription eye drops, laser therapy, or surgery to lower intraocular pressure.

Glaucoma - Closed Angle

Closed angle glaucoma results from a sudden increase in pressure due to a blockage of the drainage system inside...

Closed angle glaucoma is a medical emergency caused by a sudden blockage of the eye's drainage system, leading to rapid pressure increase.

✅ Treatment: Immediate medical attention, laser iridotomy, or surgery to relieve the blockage.

Diabetic Retinopathy

Diabetes is one of the top causes of blindness in the world occurring in both type I & type II diabetes. In the early...

Diabetic retinopathy is caused by damage to the blood vessels in the retina due to diabetes. It can lead to vision loss if untreated.

✅ Treatment: Blood sugar control, laser therapy, anti-VEGF injections, or vitrectomy.

Macular Degeneration

Macular degeneration is a disease affecting the central vision. More common in the elderly, UV protection from a young...

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) affects the macula, the part of the retina responsible for central vision.

✅ Treatment: Lifestyle changes, nutritional supplements, anti-VEGF injections, or laser therapy.

Hypertensive Retinopathy

Hypertensive retinopathy is the loss of vision due to poorly controlled high blood pressure. Worldwide this is one of the...

Hypertensive retinopathy is caused by damage to the retinal blood vessels due to high blood pressure.

✅ Treatment: Blood pressure control, regular eye exams, and lifestyle modifications.

Vitreous Floaters

Floaters are small particles that often move about within the clear jelly inside the eye. They are usually harmless but a...

Floaters are small clumps of gel or cells inside the vitreous that cast shadows on the retina. They are usually harmless but can sometimes indicate a retinal tear.

✅ Treatment: Observation, or surgery if they significantly impair vision.

Lens & Coating Options

Find the right lens design and coating for your vision needs.

Single Vision

Single Vision lenses have the same power across the entire surface. Available in a variety of...

Single vision lenses have one optical power across the entire lens surface. Used to correct either distance vision or near vision.

✅ Pros: Simple, affordable, wide field of view.
❌ Cons: Only corrects one distance.

Bifocal

Bifocal lenses are two lenses combined into one. There is a visible line between the two powers; where the upper portion...

Bifocal lenses have two optical powers separated by a visible line. The top corrects distance, the bottom is for near vision.

✅ Pros: Clear vision at two distances.
❌ Cons: Visible line, no intermediate vision.

Progressive Readers

Progressive readers are specialized lenses that provide a range of clarity for near and intermediate vision...

Progressive lenses offer a seamless transition from distance to near vision without visible lines.

✅ Pros: No visible lines, natural vision, corrects all distances.
❌ Cons: Adaptation period required.

Multifocal

Multifocal lenses are many lenses combined into one with varying progressive powers. The lens has no...

Multifocal lenses provide multiple optical powers within a single lens, allowing for clear vision at near, intermediate, and far distances.

✅ Pros: Versatile, no need to switch glasses.
❌ Cons: Adaptation period required.

Anti-reflection Coating

The purpose of an AR coating is to reduce lens surface reflections thus increasing lens transparency and provides the best...

AR coating reduces reflections on the lens surface, improving clarity and reducing eye strain. It also enhances the appearance of the lenses.

✅ Benefits: Reduced glare, improved night vision, and better cosmetic appearance.

Blue Protection Coating

The Blue Light Coating protects your eyes, reducing strain and safeguarding vision from harmful blue light emitted by...

Blue light coating filters out harmful blue light from digital screens, reducing eye strain and potentially protecting against retinal damage.

✅ Benefits: Less eye strain, better sleep quality, and long-term eye health.

Polarisation

Polarized lenses reduce glare from reflective surfaces like water, snow, and roads. They enhance visual comfort and clarity in bright conditions.

✅ Benefits: Reduced glare, improved visual comfort, and better contrast.

Variable Tint

Adaptive lenses are clear lenses that darken like sunglasses on exposure to UV light. They protect the health of the eye...

Variable tint lenses automatically darken in sunlight and return to clear indoors, providing protection from UV rays.

✅ Benefits: UV protection, convenient all-in-one solution, and comfortable vision in varying light conditions.

Prism

Misalignment or a slight discrepancy in the position of the eyes causes visual fatigue or double vision. Prismatic lenses...

Prism lenses help correct double vision or eye misalignment by bending light to align the images seen by each eye.

✅ Benefits: Reduced double vision, less eye strain, and improved visual comfort.

HRI (High Refractive Index)

HRI Higher refractive index lenses have a greater 'light bending' ability than ordinary lenses, therefore are significantly...

High Refractive Index lenses are thinner and lighter than standard lenses, making them ideal for strong prescriptions.

✅ Benefits: Thinner edges, lighter weight, and better cosmetic appearance.

Daily disposable contact lenses offer convenience and hygiene. They are worn once and discarded, reducing the risk of infection.

✅ Benefits: No cleaning, fresh lenses daily, and reduced risk of complications.

Spherical contact lenses correct myopia or hyperopia with a single power across the entire lens.

✅ Benefits: Simple design, widely available, and comfortable for daily wear.

Toric contact lenses are specially designed to correct astigmatism. They have different powers in different meridians.

Multifocal contact lenses provide clear vision at near, intermediate, and far distances, ideal for presbyopia.

Scleral lenses are large GP lenses that vault over the cornea, creating a tear-filled reservoir for comfort. Ideal for irregular corneas.

Rigid gas permeable lenses provide sharp vision by maintaining their shape on the eye. They allow oxygen to pass through.

Eyelid & Surface Conditions

Understanding common conditions affecting the eyelids and eye surface.

Blepharitis

Blepharitis is a common eyelid condition affecting people of all ages and is not contagious, causes itching, redness and...

Blepharitis is inflammation of the eyelids causing redness, itching, and flaky skin. It is not contagious.

✅ Treatment: Warm compresses, eyelid hygiene, and prescribed medications if needed.

Meibomitis

Meibomitis is an Inflammation of the small glands that produce the oily component of the tear layer that keeps...

Meibomitis is inflammation of the meibomian glands, which produce the oily layer of tears. It can cause dry eye symptoms.

✅ Treatment: Warm compresses, eyelid hygiene, and anti-inflammatory medications.

Allergic Conjunctivitis

Allergic conjunctivitis is an inflammation of the thin clear membrane that covers the white of the eye. Most commonly...

Allergic conjunctivitis is an inflammation of the conjunctiva caused by allergens like pollen, dust, or pet dander.

✅ Treatment: Antihistamine eye drops, cold compresses, and avoiding allergens.

GPC

GPC is as a result of the eye developing an allergic reaction, often associated with contact lens intolerance. Removing the...

GPC (Giant Papillary Conjunctivitis) is an allergic reaction on the inner surface of the eyelid, often associated with contact lens use.

✅ Treatment: Discontinuing lens wear, switching to different lens materials, and prescribed eye drops.

Pterygium

A pterygium is an elevated wedge shaped growth on the surface of the eye, usually on the inside corner of the eye. Exposure...

A pterygium is a fleshy growth on the conjunctiva that can extend onto the cornea. It is associated with UV exposure.

✅ Treatment: UV protection, lubricating eye drops, and surgical removal if it affects vision.

Pinguecula

A pinguecula is a small elevated mass on the white of the eye. Usually slightly yellow in appearance, caused by UV light...

A pinguecula is a yellowish growth on the conjunctiva, often caused by UV light exposure or dry environments.

✅ Treatment: UV protection, lubricating eye drops, and surgical removal if symptomatic.

Neovascularisation

Neovascularisation is a complication caused by a lack of oxygen to the cornea commonly found in contact lens over...

Neovascularisation is the growth of new blood vessels into the cornea, often due to contact lens over-wear or oxygen deprivation.

✅ Treatment: Reducing lens wear, switching to high-oxygen lenses, and regular monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to our most common questions.

How often should I get my eyes tested?
We recommend a comprehensive eye exam every 1–2 years, or more frequently if you have a pre-existing condition or notice changes in your vision. Regular check-ups help detect issues early.
Do you accept medical aid?
Yes, we work with most major medical aids. Please bring your membership card and we'll assist with the claims process. Our team can also provide guidance on your specific plan's coverage.
What should I bring to my appointment?
Please bring your current glasses or contact lenses (if applicable), your medical aid card, a form of ID, and any referral letters from your doctor. We'll take care of the rest.
Do you offer driver's certificate testing?
Absolutely. We provide driver's license and PDP (Public Driver's Permit) eye testing on site. The certificate is issued immediately after your test.
